PRD — Zoho CRM ⇌ Zoho Survey Integration
=========================================
Status: DRAFT · Problem definition phase
Author: Jay (PM) · Persona lens: Jay — Sales Rep + CRM admin


==================================================================
1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
==================================================================

Companies collect customer feedback in a survey tool and manage customer
relationships in a CRM — two systems that never talk. As a result, the
customer's VOICE (what they feel) and the customer's VALUE and CONTEXT (who
they are, what they're worth, where they are in the journey, who owns them)
live in separate worlds.

A survey answer, on its own, is an island: it knows what was said but not who
truly said it, what they're worth, or what should happen next. A CRM record, on
its own, is deaf: it knows the deal, the money, and the history, but not how the
customer actually feels about any of it.

Because the two are disconnected:
  - Feedback arrives with no business context, so no one can tell which
    responses matter (a detractor worth ₹5,000 looks identical to one worth
    ₹18,00,000).
  - Feedback never reaches the person who owns the relationship, at the place
    they work (the CRM record), so it is not acted upon in time.
  - Feedback cannot trigger anything — it ends as a static report, not a task,
    an alert, or a next step.
  - Reporting is aggregate and anonymous ("our NPS is 32") and cannot be sliced
    by the CRM dimensions that drive decisions (deal stage, rep, product,
    account, revenue).

The problem to solve: make the customer's voice a first-class, contextual,
actionable signal INSIDE the system of record — so feedback is automatically
tied to the right customer and deal, weighted by real business value, visible
to the person who owns it, and able to drive action — without anyone leaving CRM
or reconciling two tools by hand.

2. BACKGROUND — first-principles research
==================================================================

2.1 What a survey actually is
-----------------------------
A survey tool is a structured-feedback engine. Its job: build a questionnaire,
distribute it (link / email / embed / QR), collect responses, and report on
them in aggregate. The atomic output is a RESPONSE — a set of answers tied to a
respondent, captured at a single point in time.

2.2 What data a survey holds
----------------------------
  - The answers themselves (ratings, choices, open text)
  - Respondent identity — ONLY what the respondent typed, or what was passed in
    via URL/custom variables (often just a name/email, sometimes nothing)
  - Metadata — timestamp, time taken, IP/device, collector/channel, completion
    status (complete / partial / disqualified)
  - Derived metrics — NPS, CSAT, CES, star ratings, scored results
  - Aggregate views — response rate, answer distributions, cross-tabs, trends by
    collector

2.3 Who uses a survey standalone (and for what)
-----------------------------------------------
  - CX / Support — CSAT after a ticket, NPS relationship surveys
  - Sales / Account teams — post-purchase, post-demo, win/loss feedback
  - HR — employee engagement / eNPS
  - Marketing — audience research, lead-gen forms, campaign feedback
  - Product / UX — feature and usability research
  - Events — post-event attendee feedback
In every case the results sit inside the survey tool, disconnected from the
system where the customer relationship actually lives.

2.4 How survey data is INCOMPLETE on its own (the core gap)
-----------------------------------------------------------
A standalone response is missing five things it cannot supply itself:
  (a) IDENTITY   — who the respondent really is beyond a typed email; no reliable
                   link to a known customer record. Typos, anonymous responses,
                   and shared inboxes make matching unreliable.
  (b) VALUE      — what the respondent is worth. No deal amount, no pipeline, no
                   lifetime value. Every respondent is weighted equally.
  (c) CONTEXT    — where they are in the lifecycle (prospect / new buyer /
                   renewing / churning) and their history (past purchases,
                   prior complaints, previous survey answers).
  (d) OWNERSHIP  — who in the company owns this relationship and should respond.
  (e) ACTION     — the survey tool can SHOW a detractor but cannot DO anything:
                   it cannot create a task, assign an owner, escalate, or update
                   a record. The insight dies as a report.
It is also blind on AUDIENCE: it has no live view of the customer base, so it
cannot send "the right survey at the right lifecycle moment" — sending is a
manual campaign, not an event-driven act.

2.5 What a CRM actually is (and holds)
--------------------------------------
A CRM is the system of record for customer relationships. It holds identity and
full profile (contacts, accounts, leads), MONEY and STAGE (deals/potentials with
amount, pipeline stage, close/renewal date), ACTIVITY and HISTORY (calls,
emails, tasks, meetings, timeline), OWNERSHIP (which rep/team), and PRODUCTS.
Critically, it also has an ACTION ENGINE — workflows, assignment rules, tasks,
field updates, escalation — and a live, always-current, segmentable audience.

2.6 How CRM is ALSO incomplete (the reverse gap)
------------------------------------------------
A CRM is deaf. It records what the company DID (calls made, emails sent, stage
moved) but not how the customer FEELS. It has no structured, quantified,
comparable voice-of-customer — at best a free-text note ("seems unhappy"),
never a systematic NPS/CSAT captured at key lifecycle moments.

2.7 How the two complete each other
-----------------------------------
  Survey = the VOICE (sentiment, structured feedback, at a moment in time)
  CRM    = the IDENTITY, VALUE, CONTEXT, OWNERSHIP, and ACTION

Connected, each fills the other's gap:
  - CRM enriches every response — identity resolved to a real record, weighted
    by deal value, placed in lifecycle context, attributed to an owner. An
    anonymous "score of 3" becomes "a ₹18L open deal, owned by Jay, at risk."
  - Survey enriches every CRM record — the deaf record gains the customer's
    actual voice, quantified and trended, so the relationship is no longer
    managed blind.
  - CRM's action engine gives feedback consequences — a response can now create
    a task, alert an owner, flag a deal, or trigger a workflow.
  - CRM's live audience lets surveys fire at the exact lifecycle moment (deal
    delivered, service closed) automatically, at scale, instead of by hand.
The unique value is the JOIN: sentiment attached to a revenue/relationship
object, made actionable inside the system of record.

3. PROBLEMS BY PERSONA
==================================================================

3.1 Sales Rep (Jay) — problems today
------------------------------------
PR-1  Feedback lives in another tool. To see how a customer feels I must leave
      CRM, open Survey, and match the response to my contact by email myself.
PR-2  I learn a customer is unhappy too late — the feedback never surfaces on
      the record I'm actually working, so I miss the window to save the deal.
PR-3  I can't tell which feedback matters. A bad response gives me no sense of
      whether it's my biggest deal or a trivial one — the survey doesn't know
      the deal value.
PR-4  I can't see who actually responded. I don't know which of my contacts
      answered and which ignored me, so I can't chase the silent ones.
PR-5  I can't act from the feedback. Nothing is created for me — no task, no
      reminder — so following up depends on me remembering.
PR-6  Sending is manual and badly timed. I send surveys one contact at a time
      and forget to send at the right moment (right after a test drive or
      delivery), so I lose feedback I could have captured.
PR-7  No history on the record. I can't see this customer's past sentiment or
      whether it's trending down — every interaction starts blind.

3.2 Admin (Jay) — problems today
--------------------------------
PA-1  Getting survey data into CRM is manual. Export CSVs, match by email,
      re-import — stale by the time it lands, and repeated every cycle.
PA-2  Identity resolution is unreliable. Mistyped or anonymous respondents don't
      match cleanly to records, creating duplicates or orphaned responses.
PA-3  I can't automate lifecycle-timed sending. There's no way to fire a survey
      automatically on a CRM event (stage change, deal closed, service done) —
      it's always a manual campaign disconnected from what's happening in CRM.
PA-4  Feedback and revenue live in two reporting worlds. I cannot build a report
      that crosses them — "sentiment by deal stage / by rep / by product / by
      account" is impossible when the data never meets.
PA-5  Bad responses can't be routed or actioned automatically. The survey tool
      can't create a CRM task, assign an owner, or trigger a workflow, so
      closing the loop is entirely manual.
PA-6  Duplicate / incomplete records. Responses that create leads don't dedupe
      against existing CRM records, polluting the database.
PA-7  Two-tool governance overhead. Separate audience lists, separate consent /
      GDPR tracking, separate access control — double the maintenance and risk.

3.3 Sales Manager / Exec — problems today (for completeness)
------------------------------------------------------------
PM-1  An NPS/CSAT number with no revenue or accountability attached — cannot
      answer "what is this costing us" or "who owns it."
PM-2  Feedback is a lagging, aggregate, anonymous report — not a live
      operational signal that can be prioritized or delegated.
PM-3  No way to see whether acting on feedback actually moved the business,
      because sentiment and outcomes are never in the same view.

4. TARGET USERS & JOBS-TO-BE-DONE
==================================================================

Note on personas: in this integration ONE person, Jay, wears two hats — he is
both the Sales Rep who lives in the records AND the CRM admin who configures the
integration. The Sales Manager / Exec is a distinct consumer of the aggregate
view. We design for all three, but Jay is the primary lens.

JTBD format used below: "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can
[outcome]." Jobs describe the progress the user is trying to make — not features.


4.1 PRIMARY USER — Sales Rep (Jay)
----------------------------------
Profile:   Account Executive at the dealership. Lives in Zoho CRM all day; works
           deals and contacts. Strong in CRM concepts, no interest in learning a
           separate survey product. Measured on deals won and revenue.
Uses it:   Continuously, at the record level, as part of working a deal.
Cares about: Not losing a deal he could have saved; knowing what to do next.

Jobs-to-be-done:
  JR-1  When a customer gives feedback on a deal I'm working, I want that
        feedback to show up on the deal as a clear signal, so I can act on it
        before the deal is lost.
  JR-2  When feedback is negative, I want to see WHY (in the customer's words)
        and what to do next, so my recovery is fast and specific instead of
        generic.
  JR-3  When a customer hits a key moment (test drive done, car delivered), I
        want the right survey to go out automatically, so I capture feedback
        without having to remember to send it.
  JR-4  When I plan my day, I want to see which of my deals are at risk from
        customer sentiment, so I prioritize the ones worth saving.
  JR-5  When I've followed up and fixed the issue, I want the risk flag to clear
        on the record, so my effort is reflected and I know I'm done.
  JR-6  When I open a customer, I want to see their feedback history and whether
        sentiment is trending down, so I never walk into a conversation blind.

4.2 PRIMARY USER — Admin (Jay, admin hat)
-----------------------------------------
Profile:   Same person, configuring the integration for the team. Comfortable
           with CRM setup (modules, fields, workflows, assignment) but not with
           survey-specific machinery.
Uses it:   Once at setup, then occasionally to tune (thresholds, mappings).
Cares about: Automation that scales, data that lands clean, no manual glue work.

Jobs-to-be-done:
  JA-1  When a deal reaches a key stage, I want a survey to fire automatically to
        the right person, so feedback is captured at scale without manual sends.
  JA-2  When a response comes back, I want it linked to the correct deal/contact
        automatically, so I never reconcile by hand or create duplicates.
  JA-3  When I set up the integration, I want to control which metric drives risk
        (NPS/CSAT/intent) and how answers map to CRM fields, so the signal fits
        how our business actually works.
  JA-4  When feedback is negative, I want it to trigger CRM actions (task, alert,
        owner assignment), so the follow-up loop closes without manual routing.
  JA-5  When responses create records, I want them to dedupe against existing
        CRM data, so the database stays clean.

4.3 SECONDARY USER — Sales Manager / Exec
-----------------------------------------
Profile:   Owns the number and the team. Consumes aggregate reporting to make
           decisions; does not work individual records. Thinks in revenue,
           pipeline, and rep performance.
Uses it:   Periodically (e.g. weekly review), at the dashboard level.
Cares about: Exposure in ₹, root cause, and proof that action worked.

Jobs-to-be-done:
  JM-1  When I review the business, I want to see how much revenue is at risk
        from customer sentiment and how it's trending, so I know where to
        intervene.
  JM-2  When at-risk revenue rises, I want to find the systemic cause (which
        model, rep, stage, or showroom), so I fix the process, not each symptom.
  JM-3  When I spot a cluster of at-risk deals, I want to delegate or bulk-assign
        follow-ups to my team, so we act within the week.
  JM-4  When I change something (coaching, a process, a config), I want to see
        the number move, so I can prove the intervention paid off.

4.4 The end-to-end job (the through-line)
-----------------------------------------
Across all three, the overarching job is:
  "When a customer tells us how they feel, I want that voice to become a
   contextual, actionable signal on the right record — automatically — so the
   right person acts in time and we protect the revenue."
This single job travels three altitudes: Admin makes it flow, Rep acts on it,
Manager steers by it.

5. GOALS & SUCCESS METRICS / NORTH STAR
==================================================================

5.1 Goals
---------
Business goals (why the company builds this):
  BG-1  Differentiate the suite — make cross-product integration a reason to buy
        CRM + Survey together instead of best-of-breed point tools.
  BG-2  Drive adoption and retention of both products by making them more
        valuable connected than apart.
  BG-3  Help customers PROTECT revenue, giving a hard ROI story that justifies
        the spend.

Product goals (what the product must achieve for users):
  PG-1  Turn every relevant response into a contextual, actionable signal on the
        right CRM record — automatically. (addresses PR-1,2,3 / PA-1,2)
  PG-2  Close the feedback loop faster — shrink the time from "customer speaks"
        to "owner acts." (addresses PR-2,5 / PA-4)
  PG-3  Give managers a revenue-weighted, sliceable view of sentiment they can
        steer by. (addresses PM-1,2,3)
  PG-4  Make it scale with zero manual glue — event-driven sending and automatic
        record linking. (addresses PA-1,2,3)


5.2 NORTH STAR METRIC
---------------------
      Recovered At-Risk Revenue
      = Σ (Amount) of deals that were FLAGGED at-risk by a customer's voice
        AND subsequently RECOVERED (owner acted → deal won / retained /
        sentiment re-scored healthy).

Why this is the North Star:
  - It sits exactly on the value the integration creates: voice → context →
    action → revenue saved. It is impossible to move without both products.
  - It is a shared score across all three personas: Admin makes the signal flow,
    Rep acts, Manager steers — and all of it shows up here.
  - It aligns the company and the customer: we win when the customer keeps
    revenue they would otherwise have lost.

A response-only or "NPS improved" metric was rejected as North Star: it measures
the survey, not the integration, and can rise without any revenue impact.


5.3 SUPPORTING METRIC TREE
--------------------------
Grouped as a funnel from setup → the closed loop → business outcome. Targets
shown as direction (illustrative numbers marked *illus.*).

A) ADOPTION / ACTIVATION (is it set up and flowing?)
   AM-1  % of key deal stages with an auto-send survey workflow configured   ↑
   AM-2  # of surveys auto-sent via CRM events per week                       ↑
   AM-3  Identity-resolution rate — % of responses auto-linked to the correct
         record with no manual matching     ↑  (target >95% *illus.*)  [PA-2]
   AM-4  Response rate: CRM-triggered sends vs manual sends                    ↑

B) ENGAGEMENT (are the signals seen and worked?)
   EM-1  % of at-risk deals viewed by their owner                             ↑
   EM-2  Weekly active managers on the Customer Voice dashboard               ↑
   EM-3  % of at-risk deals with a follow-up action created                   ↑

C) THE CLOSED LOOP (leading indicators of the North Star)
   LM-1  Time-to-first-action on a detractor (response → task/call)           ↓
         (target <24h *illus.*)  [PR-2,5]
   LM-2  % of at-risk deals acted on within SLA                               ↑
   LM-3  % of at-risk deals RECOVERED (flag cleared / won / retained)         ↑
   LM-4  Feedback coverage — % of open deals carrying ≥1 signal-bearing
         response  ↑  ("blind revenue" shrinks)

D) BUSINESS OUTCOME (lagging, the proof)
   OM-1  Recovered At-Risk Revenue (₹)  ← North Star                          ↑
   OM-2  Win-rate delta: at-risk deals WITH intervention vs WITHOUT           ↑
   OM-3  Renewal / repurchase rate: surveyed-and-actioned vs not             ↑
   OM-4  Account CSAT/NPS trend over time (tied to accounts, not aggregate)   ↑

E) COUNTER-METRICS / GUARDRAILS (don't win these the wrong way)
   CM-1  Survey fatigue — over-send rate, opt-outs, declining response rate   ↓
   CM-2  False-risk rate — deals flagged at-risk that were actually fine
         (precision of the signal)   ↓
   CM-3  Duplicate records created from responses                             ↓
   CM-4  Rep alert overload — alerts per rep per day (keep actionable)        ↓


5.4 How the metrics ladder up
-----------------------------
  Admin configures (A) → signals flow and get seen (B) → owners act fast and
  recover deals (C) → recovered revenue and better win/renewal rates show up (D)
  → all without fatigue, false alarms, or dirty data (E).
The North Star (OM-1) is the single number the whole ladder exists to move.

6. SCOPE / MVP DEFINITION
==================================================================

6.1 MVP principle
-----------------
Ship ONE complete thread end-to-end, not many partial features. The MVP must
let the full through-line job happen once, for real: a customer's voice becomes
a contextual, actionable signal on a deal, a rep acts, and the recovery shows up
at the top. A thin vertical slice that closes the loop beats a broad set of
disconnected widgets — because the whole pitch is the loop, not any one screen.

Running example held constant: the Kia showroom, the test-drive survey, the
Deals module.


6.2 IN SCOPE (MVP)
------------------
Send & link (Admin path):
  S-1  One event-driven send: a deal-stage change fires ONE survey via workflow.
       (test-drive done → test-drive experience survey)   [JA-1 / PA-3]
  S-2  The send stamps dealId + contactId into the link (custom variables), so
       the response is DEAL-SCOPED.                        [JA-2]
  S-3  On response, it auto-links to the correct deal/contact — no manual
       matching.                                           [JA-2 / PA-2]

Signal & calculation (the model):
  S-4  One normalized sentiment score s (0-1) per response, from the survey's
       primary metric (intent for pre-sale test-drive; NPS/CSAT otherwise).
  S-5  Per-deal At-Risk Revenue = Amount x (1 - s), counted only for open deals
       below threshold. Unit of risk = the deal (no contact-total lumping).
  S-6  One signal per deal (latest / worst-active).

Manager surface (aggregate):
  S-7  Customer Voice dashboard as a CRM surface: hero At-Risk Revenue (₹) +
       count + trend.                                      [JM-1]
  S-8  Ranked at-risk deals list (by ₹), each row drillable to the record.
                                                           [JM-1 / JR-4]

Rep surface (record):
  S-9  At-risk signal panel on the Deal: badge, this deal's ₹ at risk, the
       driving score, and the verbatim "why".              [JR-1,2]
  S-10 Next Best Action on the record.                     [JR-3-equiv/JR-2]

Close the loop:
  S-11 Recovery: rep logs a follow-up / the signal re-scores healthy → the deal
       drops off the at-risk list and the manager total falls by that amount.
                                                           [JR-5 / JM-4]

MVP scope guards:
  - ONE module (Deals), ONE survey type (test-drive), ONE metric driver.
  - Numbers reconcile: manager hero = Σ of the record-level ₹.


6.3 EXPLICITLY OUT OF SCOPE (v2+)
---------------------------------
Deferred so the MVP stays a shippable thread. Named to protect the boundary:
  X-1  Segment / pattern panel (by model, consultant, showroom, stage).  [E3,E5,E6]
  X-2  Verbatim-theme clustering / root-cause themes.                    [M5]
  X-3  Relationship-scoped NPS that SPILLS across a contact's open deals.
  X-4  Multi-survey / multi-metric signal catalog & auto-detection (show-if-
       present tiles for NPS/CES/rating/score simultaneously).
  X-5  Bulk actions / delegate-to-team from the dashboard.               [JM-3]
  X-6  Real-time negative-response alerts / notifications.               [JR-4/PR-2]
  X-7  Predictive & ROI analytics: win/loss correlation, churn/renewal
       forecast, upsell whitespace.                        [E4, E8, E9]
  X-8  Full signal-model admin config UI (thresholds, metric mapping, field
       value mapping beyond what already exists).          [JA-3]
  X-9  Contacts / Accounts / Leads modules and account-level rollups.
  X-10 Auto-created tasks/cases & assignment automation on a bad response. [JA-4]

(Several of these already exist in partial form in the mock — e.g. the workflow
Send Survey action, Push-to-CRM mapping, Deal Health panel. MVP REUSES those;
it does not rebuild or expand them.)


6.4 MVP USER JOURNEY (the one thread it must complete)
------------------------------------------------------
  1. [Admin] Deal "KIA-SEL-002" stage → Test Drive Done → workflow auto-sends the
     test-drive survey to Ramesh, dealId stamped.                      (S-1,2)
  2. [System] Ramesh answers; intent 3/10 → s=0.3; response auto-links to the

  3. [System] Deal At-Risk Revenue = ₹18L x 0.7 = ₹12.6L.             (S-5)

==================================================================
7. SOLUTION OVERVIEW
==================================================================

7.1 In one line
---------------
A signal pipeline that turns a survey response into a revenue-weighted, deal-
scoped risk signal inside CRM — sent automatically on a deal event, linked back
by stamped IDs, scored to a single number s, and surfaced at two altitudes
(manager dashboard + rep record) with a recovery path that closes the loop.


7.2 The three building blocks
-----------------------------
  1. THE JOIN (plumbing) — how a response finds its deal.
     CRM stamps dealId + contactId into the survey link as custom variables at
     send; the response carries them back, so it auto-attaches to the exact deal.
     This is the foundation; nothing else works without it.

  2. THE SIGNAL MODEL (brain) — how a response becomes money-at-risk.
     Each response reduces to one normalized score s (0-1) from its primary
     metric. Per open deal: At-Risk Revenue = Amount x (1 - s). Unit of risk is
     the deal; one signal per deal (latest / worst-active). This single model is
     the source of truth both surfaces read — so numbers always reconcile.

  3. THE SURFACES (face) — where humans see and act.
     Manager: aggregate Customer Voice dashboard. Rep: at-risk panel on the deal
     record. Same model, two altitudes, guaranteed to tie out.


7.3 How it works — the pipeline
-------------------------------
  SEND    Deal-stage change → workflow fires ONE survey; IDs stamped.   (S-1,2)
    v
  LINK    Response returns with IDs → auto-attaches to the deal/contact. (S-3)
    v
  SCORE   Primary metric → s (0-1); At-Risk ₹ = Amount x (1 - s).        (S-4,5,6)
    v
  SURFACE Manager dashboard (aggregate ₹, ranked list) +
          Rep record panel (badge, ₹, driver, verbatim, NBA).           (S-7..10)
    v
  ACT     Rep works the deal from the record / manager delegates.
    v
  CLOSE   Recovery re-scores s → deal drops off → manager total falls.   (S-11)


7.4 Component breakdown
-----------------------
  C-1  Event-driven Send (Admin) — reuses the existing workflow "Send Survey"
       action; adds ID-stamping so sends are deal-scoped.
  C-2  Auto-link / Identity resolution — matches returning response to the
       record by stamped IDs (email fallback).
  C-3  Signal engine — normalizes metric → s; computes per-deal At-Risk ₹;
       enforces one-signal-per-deal and open-deal gating.
  C-4  Customer Voice dashboard (Manager) — new CRM surface: hero KPI band +
       ranked at-risk list + trend; rows drill to the record.
  C-5  At-Risk panel (Rep) — extends the existing Deal Health · Survey Signals
       panel: badge, this deal's ₹, driving score, verbatim, Next Best Action.
  C-6  Loop-close — recovery action re-scores/clears the signal and updates both
       surfaces from the same model.


7.5 Where it lives in CRM
-------------------------
  - Manager surface: a dedicated CRM dashboard ("Customer Voice"), reached from
    the CRM sidebar/nav — NOT inside the Survey module (it is a CRM decision, not
    a survey report).
  - Rep surface: on the Deal record itself, in the existing survey-signals area.
  - Admin surface: the existing workflow-rule + Push-to-CRM config screens.
This keeps the whole experience CRM-native for Jay — no second product to learn.


7.6 Mapping to features.txt
---------------------------
  MVP delivers, in full:      E1 (Revenue at Risk) + R1 (record panel) +
                              R2 (Next Best Action, basic) + R7 (event send).
  MVP reuses (already built): workflow Send Survey action, Push-to-CRM mapping,
                              Deal Health · Survey Signals panel.
  Deferred to v2+:            E2,E3,E5,E6,E7,E8,E9 (analytics/segmentation),
                              R3,R4,R5,R6,R8 (auto-tasks, list filters, alerts,
                              stakeholder coverage). See section 6.3.
The MVP is the vertical spine; features.txt is the horizontal roadmap that
builds outward from it.


7.7 Design principles for the build
-----------------------------------
  D-1  CRM-native language and placement throughout (Jay never sees survey
       jargon — no collectors/quotas in these surfaces).
  D-2  One model, two altitudes — manager total is literally Σ of record ₹.
  D-3  Reuse before rebuild — extend existing mock screens, don't duplicate.
  D-4  Show-if-present — surfaces render only the signal a survey actually has.
  D-5  Always show the "why" (verbatim) next to the "what" (score), so action is
       specific, not generic.

9.1 Solutions for the SALES REP
-------------------------------
[PR-1] Feedback lives in another tool; I match by hand
   → The customer's voice is rendered ON the deal record, auto-linked by the IDs
     stamped at send. Jay never leaves CRM or matches anything.
     (R1 / RV-1..7 · The Join, sec 7.2)

[PR-2] I learn of unhappiness too late
   → An at-risk signal + real-time negative-response alert surfaces the moment a
     bad response lands, on the record and to the owner.
     (R1, R8 / RV-1, RV-12)

[PR-3] I can't tell which feedback matters
   → Every signal is revenue-weighted: the record shows THIS deal's ₹ at risk
     (Amount x (1 - s)), so importance is in dollars, not a bare score.
     (E1, R1 / RV-2)

[PR-4] I can't see who responded vs stayed silent
   → Stakeholder coverage on the deal: which contacts answered, which are silent.
     (R6 / RV-13)

[PR-5] I can't act from the feedback
   → A bad response auto-creates a follow-up task/case assigned to the owner with
     an SLA; the record also shows a Next Best Action.
     (R2, R3 / RV-6, RV-8)

[PR-6] Sending is manual and badly timed
   → Event-triggered send: a deal-stage change auto-fires the right survey at the
     right moment (test drive done, delivered) — no remembering.
     (R7 / AV-1)

[PR-7] No sentiment history on the record
   → A sentiment trend on the record shows this response vs prior ones, so Jay
     sees direction before every conversation.
     (R1 / RV-7, RV-9)


9.2 Solutions for the ADMIN
---------------------------
[PA-1] Getting survey data into CRM is manual
   → Native, automatic sync: responses flow into CRM with no export/import, via
     Push-to-CRM field mapping.
     (Push to CRM / AV-3)

[PA-2] Identity resolution is unreliable
   → IDs (dealId/contactId) stamped into the survey link at send guarantee the
     response links to the exact record; email is a fallback, not the primary key.
     (The Join, sec 7.2 / AV-2, AV-7)

[PA-3] Can't automate lifecycle-timed sending
   → Workflow "Send Survey" action fires on any CRM event (stage change, deal
     closed, service done), so timing is automatic and rule-based.
     (R7 / AV-1)

[PA-4] Feedback and revenue live in two reporting worlds
   → The shared signal model puts sentiment on the deal, enabling reports that
     cross both — sentiment by stage / rep / product / account.
     (E3, E5, E6 / MV-4)

[PA-5] Bad responses can't be routed/actioned automatically
   → Response → CRM automation: a negative response triggers task creation,
     owner assignment, or a workflow, closing the loop without manual routing.
     (R3 / AV-4)

[PA-6] Duplicate / incomplete records
   → Duplicate-handling rule on Push-to-CRM (skip vs create-new) dedupes
     responses against existing records.
     (Push to CRM / AV-5)

[PA-7] Two-tool governance overhead
   → One integrated setup — a single audience (live CRM records), one mapping
     config, consent/ownership inherited from CRM — instead of two parallel tools.
     (Push to CRM + workflow config / AV-1..3)


9.3 Solutions for the MANAGER / EXEC
------------------------------------
[PM-1] A sentiment number with no revenue or accountability
   → Revenue at Risk + revenue-weighted sentiment: exposure in ₹, attributed to
     an owner, not a headcount-weighted score.
     (E1, E2 / MV-1)

[PM-2] Feedback is a lagging, aggregate, anonymous report
   → A live Customer Voice dashboard: ranked at-risk deals (worklist), segment
     breakdown, and objection themes — operational, not a static PDF.
     (E1, E3, E5, E6 / MV-3, MV-4, MV-5)

[PM-3] No way to see whether acting moved the business
   → Trend line + closed-loop reconciliation: when a rep recovers a deal the
     number falls; win/loss-vs-sentiment correlation proves the program's ROI.
     (E4 / MV-2, and the shared-model guarantee sec 8.4)
